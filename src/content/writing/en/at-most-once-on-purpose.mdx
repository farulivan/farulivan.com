Every system that sends something has to answer a question it would rather
avoid: when you cannot be certain a message arrived, do you send it again?

Answer yes and you have at-least-once delivery. Nothing is ever lost, and some
things arrive twice. Answer no and you have at-most-once. Nothing ever
duplicates, and occasionally something is lost. There is no third option that
is simply better; the middle ground is exactly-once, and it costs more than
most products are worth.

## The product decides, not the architecture

My reminder bot sends prayer times and habit nudges to my family's WhatsApp.
Picking a side took about a minute once I asked what each failure actually
feels like.

A missed reminder is a reminder you do not get. Mildly annoying, and the sort
of thing you notice and move on from.

A duplicated reminder is your phone buzzing twice with the same message. Do
that a few times and the bot becomes noise, and a reminder nobody reads is
worse than no reminder at all. It has trained people to ignore it.

So: at-most-once. Losing one is recoverable. Becoming ignorable is not.

That is not a general truth. A payment confirmation goes the other way without
hesitating. Send it twice, apologise for the duplicate email, never leave
someone wondering whether their money moved. The right answer is a property of
what you are sending, not of your infrastructure.

## One statement

The mechanism is smaller than the decision:

```sql
UPDATE reminders
SET sent_at = now()
WHERE id IN (
  SELECT id FROM reminders
  WHERE sent_at IS NULL AND due_at <= now()
  FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED
)
RETURNING *;
```

The row is marked sent by the same statement that selects it. Two things follow.

A restart mid-tick re-scans only rows that are genuinely unsent, because
anything already claimed has a timestamp. And two ticks that overlap cannot
claim the same row, because `SKIP LOCKED` sends the second one past anything
the first is holding rather than making it wait.

No queue, no lock table, no distributed coordinator. The database already knew
how to do this.

## Where the loss lives

Being precise about it: the row is marked sent _before_ WhatsApp confirms
delivery. If the process dies in that gap, the reminder is gone and nothing
will retry it.

That is the whole cost of the choice, and it is one line in a decision record
rather than a surprise somebody finds later. Writing it down is most of what
makes it a decision instead of an accident. The alternative is a future me
reading this code, seeing the gap, assuming it is a bug, and "fixing" it into
duplicates.
